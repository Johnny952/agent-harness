# T-089 — An epic closes when its tasks are merged or dropped

Decisions: `docs/decisions.md` ADR 134. It covers:
- settled, meaning merged or dropped, and a deferred task keeping its epic
  open;
- `done` when one task merged, and `dropped` when none did;
- the close that `accept` runs after a drop, outside the child's lock.

## What was built

- `dispatcher/tasks.py`:
  - `is_settled` and `EPIC_ALL_DROPPED_STATUS`.
  - `close_epic` counts a dropped task as settled. It closes the epic as
    `done` when one of its tasks merged, and as `dropped` when none did.
    Every other status keeps the epic open, `deferred` included.
  - `accept` runs `close_epic` after it drops a task of an epic, once its
    locks are let go. A no-op drop runs it too. The result has
    `epic_closed` only when the epic was closed.
  - The module docstring and the docstrings of `close_epic` and `accept`.
- Tests:
  - `tests/dispatcher/test_tasks.py`: a dropped task next to a merged one
    closes the epic as `done`, and two dropped tasks close it as `dropped`.
    A sibling that is pending, in progress, blocked, deferred, proposed, or
    `done` without a merge keeps it open and writes nothing.
  - `tests/dispatcher/test_board_writes.py`:
    - A deferred task holds the epic open until it is dropped and the rest
      merge.
    - Dropping every task of an accepted epic drops the epic, with
      `epic_closed` in the result.
    - A retried drop makes the close a crash cut off.
    - The close runs while another thread can take the child's lock and
      the create lock.
- Full suite: 3032 passed, 10 skipped.

## Why this way

- A dropped task is final, so it can never merge. Counting it as settled
  is the only way its epic can close.
- A deferred task is a decision the person has not made yet. Closing its
  epic would strand it, since a task of a closed epic cannot be accepted
  or run. Dropping it settles it, and now also closes the epic.
- `dropped` for an epic none of whose tasks merged: nothing of it shipped,
  and no tool could move it later.
- The trigger runs after `accept`'s locks are let go, so `close_epic` takes
  the parent's lock under no child's, as ADR 129 requires.
