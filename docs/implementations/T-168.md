# T-168 — hold an expired task lock while an orphan phase may run

Decision: `docs/decisions.md` ADR 411.

## What was built

- `_phase_may_be_running` and `orphan_holders` in `dispatcher/dispatcher.py`.
  A phase may be running until `phase_timeout_seconds + heartbeat_ttl_seconds`
  after the later of its `busy_since` and the card's heartbeat; no claim stamp
  counts as running.
- `reap_stale_busy_accounts` keeps an expired-heartbeat holder inside that
  window. Without this the reaper frees the account first and the guard sees
  nothing (both reapers run before the claim).
- `reap_expired_locks` skips a task with an orphan holder and logs one line.
- `tasks.acquire_lock(orphan_holders=...)` raises
  `context_transfer.OrphanPhaseError` (a `LockHeldError`) in the takeover
  branch, before any write. `dispatch_phase` passes the holders of the task
  other than the account it claimed; its existing `except` gives that account
  back. `run_phase` logs a warning naming the account instead of a traceback.

## Why this way

The window is more conservative than the Done-when's "within
`phase_timeout_seconds` of its claim": a long phase whose dispatcher died just
after a heartbeat leaves a claude that can run a full timeout past that beat.

## Ruled out

A `docker exec pgrep` per holder (cost on every account pick; argv does not
name the task). Killing the orphan (out of scope).

## Tests

`tests/dispatcher/test_dispatcher.py`: predicate, `reap_expired_locks` (same
task refused, other task taken, past the window taken), `acquire_lock`,
the reaper, `dispatch_phase` on three cases, and `run_task_cycle` logging.
Three existing reaper tests were re-aged past the window
(`_ORPHAN_WINDOW_PAST`); their assertions are unchanged.
