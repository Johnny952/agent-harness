# T-020 — the primary's week is paced against its own reset

`docs/plans/token-economy.md` **P8**, built. One number held two windows:
`quota.exceeds_threshold` compared `reserve_pct` (60) with `session_pct` and
`week_pct` alike, and against a weekly counter that refills on a date, 60 is
too loose on day one and too strict on day seven. Both were observed — cuenta1
was parked at 84% of a week that reset the next day, so roughly 40% of it
expired unspent.

The decision is `docs/decisions.md` **ADR 48**, appended by this task's
arquitecto, and everything here is transcription of it. It narrows ADR 20 and
ADR 18 without superseding either, and `docs/charter.md` **C-2** — the reserve
is "a ceiling, not a partition" — is the ruling that makes pacing allowed at
all, and the one quoted rather than re-decided.

Scope: `dispatcher/`, `tests/dispatcher/`, `config.example.yaml`, `README.md`,
`docs/`. Nothing under `observability/`, `front/` or `docker/` moved, and
`config.yaml` and `docs/charter.md` are untouched. No probe ran against a live
container: every test here is recorded `/usage` text with its clock supplied.

## What was built

### Two ceilings, and which window each governs

`dispatcher/quota.py` grew the whole rule, all of it pure and clock-free:

- `parse_reset(clause, now)` turns the week line's clause into an aware
  datetime. It accepts `%b %d, %I:%M%p` and `%b %d, %I%p` followed by a zone
  in parentheses, and answers `None` on everything else.
- `weekly_ceiling_pct(days_left)` is ADR 48's ramp before the margin: 10% while
  more than `PACE_RAMP_DAYS` remain, `10 + 85 × (6 − d) / 6` through the ramp,
  95% from one day out. P8's table of 10/24/38/53/67 is this function rounded.
- `week_ceiling(week_reset, now, reserve_pct)` returns a `WeekCeiling` — the
  `pct` the week is compared with (margin already off), the `days_left`, the
  `reset` it read, and a `fallback_reason` when it could not pace. Nothing
  caches one: it recomputes from the clause it is handed, which is what keeps
  the ceiling from stepping at midnight and what lets a parked primary be
  released by the ceiling rising.

`exceeds_threshold` was left exactly as it was. It is still the whole rule for
a worker, and for the primary with the pacing switched off, so changing it
would have made one function mean two things.

The primary's session keeps `reserve_pct`, and ADR 48 says why rather than
leaving it as inertia: C-2's reason for the reserve is that a fallback phase
must not spend the console to the wall, because the operator then loses the
thread that would notice — and that is about the window a phase runs in, not
about the one that refills on a date. No ADR argued for pacing the session.
`_load_reserve_pct`'s invariant therefore still governs something real.

### Five constants, and why none of them is a key

`PACE_START_PCT` (10), `PACE_FINAL_PCT` (95), `PACE_RAMP_PCT` (85),
`PACE_RAMP_DAYS` (6) and `PACE_PROBE_MARGIN_PCT` (5) sit beside the parser. The
ramp's shape is a decision, not a dial, and a ramp an operator can edit is five
more numbers to reconcile with `reserve_pct`. The margin is a placeholder for a
measurement (P5) rather than a preference, so replacing it with a measured
phase cost should be one edit and not a config migration. One test asserts the
single relation between them: `PACE_RAMP_PCT` is
`PACE_FINAL_PCT - PACE_START_PCT`.

### The parser's `None` cases, and why `None` is safe

`None` for: a missing clause (the CLI omits it from a 0% line), a shape outside
the two formats, a clause with no parenthesised zone, a zone this machine
cannot resolve, and a reset already in the past. On `None` the primary's week
falls back to `reserve_pct`, which is the pre-ADR-48 behaviour exactly — so a
parse failure can delay work but can never wave through a week the older code
would have parked. A test asserts both halves of that: 70% of a week with no
reset clause still parks, 50% still passes.

Two details the plan did not anticipate:

- **The year is appended to the clause, not `replace`d onto the parsed value.**
  `strptime` defaults to 1900, which is not a leap year, so parsing first and
  substituting the year afterwards loses a `Feb 29` clause outright. Parsing
  `f"{when} {year}"` against `f"{fmt} %Y"` has no such hole.
- **There is no `now.year - 1` candidate.** The plan called for one, to carry a
  `Dec 31` clause probed on Jan 1. It cannot: the window is forward-only, so a
  last-year date is always behind `now` and could never be chosen. The test
  that was going to prove it now records the real behaviour instead — such a
  clause reads as unparseable and the week falls back, which is the safe
  direction and is why the omission costs nothing.

UTC is resolved without touching `zoneinfo`, because every recorded week line
this harness has seen reads `(UTC)` and a container image with no tz database
would otherwise lose the ramp to a missing key. Anything else goes through
`ZoneInfo` inside `except (KeyError, ValueError, OSError)`, the tuple
`docs/learnings/a-lookup-that-never-raises-catches-valueerror.md` prescribes
(`ZoneInfoNotFoundError` is a `KeyError`).

### One decision, two readers

`dispatcher._quota_decision` is now the only place in the module where a probe
is compared with a ceiling, and both `check_quota_ok` and
`_recheck_cooling_accounts` call it. That is not tidiness: the gate and the
recheck are the same decision seen twice, and with a ceiling that *moves*, two
expressions of one rule is a flap waiting for the day they disagree.
`_threshold_for`'s docstring already explained why they must agree and now says
that both reach it through `_quota_decision`, and that for the primary its
number is the session ceiling and the week's fallback rather than the week's
ceiling.

`test_the_gate_and_the_recheck_are_one_decision_about_the_ceiling` pins the
property directly: against the same probe and the same clock that parked it,
the recheck must not hand the account back. Its neighbour
`test_the_paced_primary_is_released_when_the_ceiling_rises_past_its_week`
proves the other half — the release happens at a day and a half out and not at
five days, with no new scheduler behind it.

`_utc_now()` exists only so a test can replace the clock. The ceiling moves
with the calendar, so without it every test here would have depended on the day
it ran on; the recheck's own `time.time()` is untouched, because that is the
cooldown's clock and not this one.

### The park log

The warning that parks a paced primary names the ceiling, the days left and
the reset it read:

```
account cuenta1 is at 10% of its session and 40% of its week, over the 60%
session reserve or the 19.2% weekly ceiling (5.0 days to the 2026-10-14 12:00
UTC reset it read); parking it PRE_COOLDOWN and looking for another
```

A number that moves is not explained by the number alone. The fallback logs a
separate line naming the account, the reserve and which failure it was, emitted
inside `_quota_decision` so that the gate and the recheck both report it and
neither repeats the sentence.

### The switch

`Config.pace_primary_week`, default `True`, read as
`bool(raw.get("pace_primary_week", True))` beside `gates_enabled`. No loader of
its own: unlike `reserve_pct` there is no other key to check it against. It is
last in `Config` and defaulted, so no test's `_make_config` needed editing —
`docs/learnings/config-a-new-key-is-two-edits.md` is triggered by a *required*
field. `false` restores the flat reserve on both of the primary's windows, and
a test asserts exactly that, with the numbers that park a paced primary.

### Three stale comments, and one surface

- `UsageInfo`'s docstring: nothing still *schedules* off a reset, and the
  session's clause is still read by nothing — but the week's is read on every
  probe now, so the sentence is narrowed rather than deleted. The paragraph
  about why a missing reset must not cost the percentages stays: that reasoning
  is live and is why the fallback exists.
- `_recheck_cooling_accounts`' opening comment: recovery is still a re-probe
  and not a scheduled expiry, and now that is also what releases a primary
  parked by the ceiling.
- `AccountReport.is_primary`'s comment: two ceilings, named.
- `pick_idle_account`'s fallback warning keeps both numbers it already logged —
  `reserve_pct` is still the admission line that branch is about — and names
  the week's ceiling in one clause rather than computing it, because that
  function holds no probe.
- `operator.format_status` prints `session 60%, week ceiling 47.5%` for a paced
  primary, `session 60%, week 60% — no reset read` when it fell back, and
  `(threshold 90%)` for a worker, unchanged. It calls `quota.week_ceiling`
  directly rather than `_quota_decision`, because `status` writes nothing and
  logs nothing. `format_status` grew an optional `now` for the same reason
  `_utc_now` exists — and it keeps it where `_quota_decision`'s went (see
  *Round 2* below): `operator.py` has no module-level clock seam, every
  neighbouring reader there (`account_reports`, `task_reports`,
  `release_account`) already takes a `now`, and the three paced-row tests pass
  it. One listing also has to take the clock *once*, so two rows cannot
  disagree, which a per-call `datetime.now` would not guarantee.

## What was ruled out

- **Pacing the session too.** ADR 48 says why not, and C-2's reason for the
  reserve is about the window a phase runs in. No ADR argued for it.
- **Changing `exceeds_threshold`.** It is still the entire rule for a worker
  and for an unpaced primary; teaching it about a second ceiling would have
  made one function mean two things and left the worker path paying for it.
- **Making the ramp configurable.** Five keys to reconcile with `reserve_pct`,
  for a shape that is a decision. See above.
- **Scheduling a wake at the reset.** The recheck loop already probes every
  parked account while none is IDLE, so the ceiling rising is enough and
  nothing new has to hold a timer.
- **Caching a `WeekCeiling` across probes.** The whole point is that it moves;
  a cached one steps when the cache expires rather than when the week does.
- **Editing `scripts/configure.sh`.** `grep -n "reserve\|primary"` over it
  returns nothing: the wizard writes `quota_threshold_pct` and no primary or
  reserve key at all, so a pacing switch there would be the first line it ever
  wrote about the primary. Home 2 of
  `docs/learnings/a-config-key-has-three-homes.md` is deliberately skipped, and
  this paragraph is so the next reader does not take it for an omission.
- **Rewriting P8's own bullets.** P8 has no "done" marker shape to copy — P6's
  bold *Checked* line is a measurement, not a completion — so the plan got its
  Status paragraph fixed (the one out-of-cycle edit) and one closing paragraph
  appended to the section this task finished. The plan's build order also
  claimed P1 "landed by hand as ADR 47"; it did not. ADR 47 is the
  one-operation-per-Bash-call rule, and `grep -rn "Independent reads"
  dispatcher/` finds nothing, so the Status paragraph says P1–P7 are still
  proposals.

## What is left

One row of debt, for the auditor to file: `/api/accounts` serves `reserve_pct`
on every row and the console's pool screen labels it the primary's ceiling
(ADR 18, ADR 20). Since ADR 48 that column is the primary's *session* ceiling
and its weekly fallback, and nothing serves the paced value. It bites a task
that changes `/api/accounts`, or any screen reading `reserve_pct` as the number
the primary parks on. The honest fix is one task that persists a probe and
serves both `week_pct` and the paced ceiling — the probe's own numbers have no
server-side home either. T-020 could not do it: `observability/` and `front/`
are outside its card, and `dispatcher/operator.py`'s `status` listing is the
one surface an operator reads without a browser, which is why that one is
correct in the meantime.
