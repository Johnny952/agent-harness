# T-136-D1 — `mark_orphaned` and `drop_promoted` write inbox entries without the carry lock

**Origin:** introduced, by T-136; declared by its arquitecto and implementador.
**Status:** open.
**Ruling:** accepted by T-136's revisor.
**Card:** none — nothing mirrors a row (`docs/decisions.md` ADR 59).
**Impact:** risk.

## What

`dispatcher/learnings.py` `carry` now reads and claims inbox entries under
`_carry_lock`, the per-project `flock` of ADR 294. The two other writers of
the same entries, `mark_orphaned` (rewrites an entry's frontmatter when its
carrier's cycle ends without filing) and `drop_promoted` (moves an entry out
of the inbox once the branch that filed it merges), still read and write
without that lock. ADR 294's Consequences name the gap.

## Where it bites

A task that changes who writes `carried_by`, that lets one task's
`close_cycle` or merge touch entries another task carries, or that adds a
fourth writer of inbox entries. Today each of the two touches only its own
task's entries, which a concurrent `carry` leaves alone while that task is a
live carrier, so no two writers reach the same file.

One narrow overlap exists already, found by T-136's auditor and not ruled
on by its revisor: `mark_orphaned` also rewrites an entry the dying task
*wrote* (`entry.task`), demoting its `status`, even when another task
carries it. If that carrier is itself dead, a third task's `carry` can
re-stamp the same file at the same moment, and one of the two whole-file
writes is lost.

## Why it stayed

Out of T-136's surface: the card asked for the carry's read-and-claim, and
the two writers are safe under the current ownership rule.

## Cost of leaving it

A lost update on `carried_by` (or an entry moved while it is being
re-stamped) if a future change lets two of these writers touch one entry
at once.

## Fix

Take `learnings._carry_lock` for each entry's project in both functions, or
replace the per-project lock with one lock per learnings root that all three
writers take. ADR 294 decision 1 is the lock to reuse.
