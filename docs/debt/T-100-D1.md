# T-100-D1 — `Kind.handler` in `actions.KINDS` stays `None` and unused

**Origin:** introduced, declared by T-100's arquitecto and implementador.
**Status:** resolved 2026-10-19 by T-101 (see *How it was resolved*).
**Ruling:** accepted by the revisor in round 1: ADR 148 decides it, and
`dispatcher/actions.py` must not import the dispatcher core.
**Card:** none — nothing mirrors a row (`docs/decisions.md` ADR 59).

## What

`dispatcher/actions.py` declares `Kind` with a `handler` field, from ADR 142
decision 6. Every entry of `KINDS` leaves it `None`. The handlers T-100 built
live in `dispatcher/worker.py` `HANDLERS` (`start`, `cancel`), as
`docs/decisions.md` ADR 148 decision 1 chose, because a handler calls
`dispatcher.dispatcher` and `actions.py` must stay free of the core.

## Where it bites

A task adding a worker handler for a new action kind (`command_verdict`,
`turn`), or editing `actions.Kind` or `actions.KINDS`.

## Why it stayed

The T-100 card kept `actions.py` to the step 6 fix, and ADR 148 already
records where handlers go.

## Cost of leaving it

A dead field that suggests handlers register in `KINDS`: a reader may wire a
new handler there, where the worker never looks.

## Fix

Remove `Kind.handler` and reword ADR 142 decision 6 through a superseding ADR,
so `worker.HANDLERS` is the one registry. Until then, follow ADR 148.

## How it was resolved

T-101 took the fix as declared. `Kind.handler` is removed from
`dispatcher/actions.py`, and `tests/dispatcher/test_actions.py` no longer
asserts it is `None`; `typing.Callable` stays imported, since `validate` and
`serial_key` still use it. `docs/decisions.md` ADR 179 supersedes the words
"its handler" in ADR 142 decision 6 and the `Kind.handler` sentence of ADR 148
decision 1, so `worker.HANDLERS` is the one registry of a kind's handler and
`KINDS` carries only its contract. `dispatcher/worker.py` needed no change:
nothing in it read the slot.
